45 Hard privacy policy

45 Hard is a habit-challenge app for iPhone made by Hasp Labs LLC. This page says what the app stores, who can see it, and how to get it deleted.

Effective 14 September 2026.

The short version

  • We store what you put into the app: your account, your challenges, your daily logs, your progress photos, and your friends.
  • Nothing you log is public by default. Friends see what your sharing settings allow; anyone else sees nothing unless you turn Public profile on.
  • We do not sell your data, show ads, or run analytics or tracking software in the app.
  • You can delete your account and everything in it by emailing contact@hasplabs.com.

What we store

Your account
You sign in with Apple or with an email address and password. We store the sign-in identity (your email address, or the private relay address Apple gives us if you hide it) and, for a password account, a hashed password. We never see the password itself.
Your profile
A display name and a handle you choose, your time zone (taken from your phone, used to decide what "today" means for you), and your preferences: reminder time, water increments, and notification choices.
Your challenges and logs
The challenges you create or join, the goals in them, and each day's entries: whether a goal was done, an amount where a goal has one (water, workout minutes, reading minutes, steps), and the result of each day. Where you have set up a custom goal, we store its name and target.
Progress photos
If a challenge includes a progress photo goal, the photo you take each day is uploaded to private storage under your account. Photos are JPEG files of at most 5 MB, one per day.
Steps from Apple Health
If a challenge includes a steps goal and you allow it, the app reads your daily step count from Apple Health and stores that day's total against the goal. The app only ever reads step count; it never writes to Health and reads nothing else from it. You can revoke this at any time in the Health app under Sharing.
Friends and sharing
Friend requests and friendships, the challenges you share with friends, cheers you send and receive, the sharing settings you choose, the people you block, and the reports you send.
Notifications
If you allow notifications, we store the push token your phone gives us so we can send friend requests, acceptances, invites, cheers and day results. Turn categories off in Settings inside the app, or turn notifications off for 45 Hard in iOS Settings.

We do not collect your location, your contacts, or your device identifiers, and the app contains no advertising or analytics software.

Who can see what

Five settings on the Sharing screen decide what other people see of you. Challenges, Calendar, Today's goals and History each take Nobody, Friends or Everyone, and default to Friends. Progress photos takes Off or Friends, and defaults to Off. Public profile is off by default; while it is off, a setting of Everyone behaves like Friends, and people who are not your friends can find your handle and name but nothing else.

A challenge you share with other people is a board: every participant sees every other participant's days, standings, feed lines, today's goals, attempts and badges for that challenge, whatever the sharing settings say. The sharing settings govern how people see you as a person, outside a shared challenge.

A progress photo can be seen only by you, and by a friend only when Progress photos is on, only if that friend can also see your Today's goals, and only for the current day. Friends receive a link that expires after five minutes; your own links last an hour. A photo is never public.

Blocking someone is immediate and works both ways: you disappear from each other's lists and searches, requests and cheers between you are refused, and notifications between you stop. The person you block is not told. A challenge you are both already in keeps both of you on its board.

Reports

If someone reports a profile or a photo, we receive who reported whom, the kind of report, the reason, any note they wrote, and for a photo report the location of the photo. We read reports to act on them, which may include looking at the reported content, removing a photo, or closing an account. Reports are not visible to anyone in the app.

Services that handle your data

  • Supabase hosts the database, sign-in, file storage and server functions, in the United States (AWS, Oregon).
  • Apple provides Sign in with Apple, delivers push notifications to your phone, and distributes the app through TestFlight and the App Store.
  • Expo builds the app and relays push notifications to Apple's notification service. A push token and the text of each notification pass through Expo's push service.

These providers process data on our behalf to run the app. We do not give your data to anyone else, and we do not sell it.

How long we keep it

Everything is kept while your account exists so that your history and your friends' shared challenges keep working. When you delete your account, your profile, challenges, logs, photos, friendships, sharing settings and push tokens are deleted with it. Copies may persist in our hosting provider's routine backups for a short time before they expire. We may keep a record of a report after the accounts involved are gone.

Deleting your account, and other requests

Email contact@hasplabs.com from the address on your account and ask us to delete it. We delete the account and everything tied to it within 30 days and confirm by reply. You can also ask us what we hold about you or ask us to correct it. Signing out of the app does not delete anything.

Children

45 Hard is not directed at children under 13, and we do not knowingly collect information from them. If you believe a child has created an account, email us and we will delete it.

Changes

If this policy changes, the new version is posted here with a new effective date. A change that affects what friends can see of you is also announced in the app.
